Methodology: how we calculated this

Formulas. Anomaly = value − mean of that calendar month. OLS slope b = Σ(t−t̄)(y−ȳ) / Σ(t−t̄)². t-statistic = b / SE(b) with n−2 degrees of freedom, two-tailed. 95% CI = b ± t(0.975, n−2) × SE(b), and the shaded band is the confidence interval of the fitted line. Theil-Sen = median of all pairwise slopes. Mann-Kendall S = Σ sign(yⱼ − yᵢ) with tie-corrected variance, Z from the normal approximation, τ = S / (n(n−1)/2). The meter and badge use the larger (more cautious) of the two p-values. Each briefing card names the real NASA dataset to use; the demo series themselves are synthetic.

Each month’s value has its long-term monthly average subtracted, which removes the seasonal cycle. The anomalies get a straight-line fit (OLS, with a shaded 95% confidence band), a Theil-Sen slope (median of all pairwise slopes, resistant to outliers), a t-test on the slope, and a Mann-Kendall test. The meter uses the more cautious of the two p-values: below 0.05 is significant, below 0.01 is highly significant, and anything else is inconclusive. Only linear trends are tested, and strong autocorrelation can make p-values too optimistic.
